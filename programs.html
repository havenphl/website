mission

Our mission is to nurture the success of the whole family on the journey towards stable housing through joyful child enrichment that alleviates caregiver burdens.

needs assessment

What families told us they needed

In November 2025, we spoke with caregivers at Jane Addams Place about what would be most helpful for their children and families. Their responses helped shape HAVEN’s programming:

  • 100% said their children needed more support with schoolwork
  • 88% said after-school programming would make it easier to work, attend training, or make appointments
  • 88% said their children needed more opportunities for supervised physical activity
  • 88% wanted more positive, constructive activities for their children
who we serve

Whole families, not just kids.

Supporting the child, teen, and caregiver aren’t three programs. They’re the same one.

Elementary-aged children
We provide consistent academic, social-emotional, and creative enrichment through literacy, movement, storytelling, and the arts.
High-school youth
We create paid near-peer mentorship and leadership opportunities for teens living in shelter, giving them structured ways to build confidence, communication skills, and relationships with younger children.
Caregivers
By providing reliable after-school supervision, HAVEN gives caregivers more flexibility to work, attend training, make appointments, and take other steps toward stable housing.
three prongs

Three ways kids learn, play, and create at HAVEN.

Across the week, children take part in storytelling, movement, and art — each offering a different way to learn, play, connect with others, and express themselves.

storytelling

Reading, writing, and telling stories help children strengthen literacy skills while building imagination, vocabulary, and confidence expressing their own ideas. Through read-alouds, story mapping, and creating their own books, children practice both understanding stories and becoming storytellers themselves.

reading together story mapping making our own books

movement

Regular movement supports physical health, attention, emotional regulation, and social development. Through cooperative games, dance, and choreography, children get time to be active while practicing teamwork, communication, and how to participate in a group.

cooperative games dance & choreography active play

art

Art gives children another way to explore ideas, emotions, and experiences that may be difficult to put into words. Open studio activities and guided art therapy support creativity, self-expression, fine-motor development, and opportunities to slow down and focus.

our afternoons

What actually happens, hour by hour.

As a supervised enrichment program, HAVEN addresses urgent gaps in literacy, prosocial play, and emotional development for children grades K–5, while simultaneously creating meaningful leadership pathways for high-school youth and alleviating caregiver burdens.

Mondays

  1. 3:30 – 4:20 session A Arts Creation

    Loosen up with paint, clay, and paper. No prompt, no wrong answers.

  2. 4:20 – 4:30Transition
  3. 4:30 – 5:30 session B Generative Literacy

    Reading out loud together, then writing and drawing our own — building toward each child’s book.

  4. 5:30Child Pickup

Tuesdays

  1. 3:30 – 4:20 session A Open Gym

    Room to run. Cooperative games, parachute, tag — whatever burns off the school day.

  2. 4:20 – 4:30Transition
  3. 4:30 – 5:30 session B Kardon Center for Arts Therapy

    Guided sessions with a trained art therapist. Some feelings are easier to paint than to say.

  4. 5:30Child Pickup

Thursdays

  1. 3:30 – 4:20 session A Homework

    A quiet table, a grown-up beside you, and someone to check it when you’re done.

  2. 4:20 – 4:30Transition
  3. 4:30 – 5:30 session B Dance/Movement

    Rhythm games and choreography built together — learning to move as a group.

  4. 5:30Child Pickup
