A soft place to land,
after school.

HAVEN nurtures the success of the whole family on the journey towards stable housing — through joyful child enrichment that alleviates caregiver burdens.

We're an after-school program built right inside a family shelter. Three afternoons a week, kids in grades K–5 get to draw, move, and tell their own stories — and their caregivers get a few reliable hours to work on what comes next.

about us

The home for arts, voices, enrichment, and nurturing.

In 2024, nearly a third of Philadelphians without housing were families with children. When a family lands in a shelter, the building is built for beds and paperwork — not for childhood. School lets out, and there's nowhere for a seven-year-old to be a seven-year-old.

HAVEN takes that gap seriously. We turn a few hours of the shelter's afternoon into a room that belongs to the kids: art on the tables, music on, room to run, and grown-ups who know their names. Nothing is graded. Nothing gets corrected. What a child makes is theirs.

And while their child is with us, a caregiver has something rare — time. Time for a housing appointment, a job interview, a phone call that can't happen with a five-year-old on their lap. Supporting the child and supporting the caregiver aren't two programs. They're the same one.

We started small on purpose, at one shelter, with one cohort — and we're documenting every step so other shelters can run this too.

we asked first

We built this from what families told us.

Before we designed a single session, we sat down with caregivers at Jane Addams Place and asked what would actually help. Here's what they said, in November 2025:

  • 100% said their kids needed more help with schoolwork
  • 88% said after-school programming would let them work, train, or make appointments
  • 88% said their kids needed more supervised physical activity
  • 88% wanted more positive, constructive things for their kids to do
what we do

Three threads, woven into every afternoon.

Sessions aren't a schedule of separate classes. Storytelling, movement, and art keep folding into each other — a story becomes a dance, a dance becomes a drawing.

storytelling

We read out loud together — stopping to ask questions, argue with the characters, and wonder what happens next. Then the kids write and draw their own. By the end of the semester, every child has a book with their name on the cover.

reading together story mapping making our own books

movement

Shelter life is short on room to run. So we make room — cooperative games, parachute days, tug-of-war, and choreography built with CityStep. It's how kids shake off a long day and learn to work with each other at the same time.

cooperative games dance & choreography settling down together

art

Open studio and guided art therapy sessions with the Kardon Center. Some feelings are easier to paint than to say out loud, and a kid who's had a hard week gets to put it somewhere. Materials are good ones, and there's no wrong way to use them.

an afternoon at HAVEN

What actually happens, hour by hour.

We meet Mondays, Tuesdays, and Thursdays, 3:30 to 5:45. The is the same every time — a lighter warm-up block, a short breather, then the main event. Kids who've had a lot of upheaval shouldn't have to guess what's coming next.

Mondays

  1. 3:30 – 4:15 warm-up Arts creation

    Loosen up with paint, clay, and paper. No prompt, no wrong answers.

  2. 4:20 – 4:30A breather
  3. 4:30 – 5:30 main block Storytelling & writing

    Reading out loud together, then writing and drawing our own — building toward each child's book.

  4. 5:30 – 5:45Pickup

Tuesdays

  1. 3:30 – 4:15 warm-up Open gym

    Room to run. Cooperative games, parachute, tag — whatever burns off the school day.

  2. 4:20 – 4:30A breather
  3. 4:30 – 5:30 main block Art therapy

    Guided sessions with a trained art therapist. Some feelings are easier to paint than to say.

  4. 5:30 – 5:45Pickup

Thursdays

  1. 3:30 – 4:15 warm-up Homework help

    A quiet table, a grown-up beside you, and someone to check it when you're done.

  2. 4:20 – 4:30A breather
  3. 4:30 – 5:30 main block Dance & movement

    Rhythm games and choreography built together — learning to move as a group.

  4. 5:30 – 5:45Pickup

Every session starts with hellos and a snack — nobody learns anything hungry. Four adults are in the room the whole time: two program leads, two undergraduate mentors, and a paid high-school intern from the shelter itself. Our team stays after pickup to talk through how the day went.

who we serve

Whole families, not just enrolled kids.

  • ★Children in grades K–5 living at the shelter — about 40 of them across two semesters.
  • ★Around 30 caregivers, who get consistent, predictable hours back to work toward housing and employment.
  • ★About 10 high-school interns each semester, hired from within the shelter and paid for their work with the younger kids.
  • ★Shelter staff and case managers, who we work alongside rather than around.

Enrollment is free. Families move a lot; a child who leaves and comes back is still ours.

We don't do this alone.

HAVEN was built with the families and staff of Jane Addams Place, and we work with the Kardon Center, CityStep, Tree House Books, and Mural Arts Philadelphia. Dr. Caroline Watts of Penn's Graduate School of Education advises the program, and Penn's Early Childhood and Family Research Center is helping us measure whether it works.
